Volatility (risk)

How much the price jumps around. I take the daily price changes, measure how spread out they are (the standard deviation), and scale it up to a yearly number by multiplying by the square root of 365, since crypto trades every day.

Higher volatility means a bumpier ride: bigger gains on good days and bigger losses on bad days.

Return per unit of risk. It takes the average yearly return, subtracts the risk-free rate, and divides by volatility. Higher is better. Below 0 means you would have done better in Treasury bills.

Why can Ethereum lose money but still have a positive Sharpe ratio ()? The Sharpe ratio uses the simple average of daily returns. Big swings hurt the final result more than the average shows: a 50% drop followed by a 50% gain averages 0%, but leaves you 25% down. This gap is called volatility drag, and it is one more cost of high risk.

Correlation

How closely two coins move together, from -1 to 1. Close to 1 means they usually go up and down on the same days. Close to 0 means no connection.

Stablecoin

A coin designed to stay at a fixed price, usually $1. Tether (USDT) is one of the most widely used. I include it as a baseline: it shows what "almost no risk" looks like in this data.

Log scale

A chart scale where each step up is the same percentage change, not the same dollar change. Going from $10 to $20 looks the same as $1,000 to $2,000. It is useful when prices change a lot.

About the data

Daily closing prices and volumes for days (Oct 1, 2021 to Sep 30, 2026), downloaded from Yahoo Finance. The window is fixed so anyone who re-runs the code gets the same numbers. All calculations are in src/analysis.py.

Limits: past results don't predict future prices; five coins are not the whole market; and the period starts close to the November 2021 peak, which makes the results look worse than a window starting a year earlier.
